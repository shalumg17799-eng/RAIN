Overview
This document specifies the Rural Health AI Infrastructure for Nevada (RAIN) prototype, developed by the University of Nevada, Reno in partnership with Radiant Digital. The prototype demonstrates how a unified AI intelligence layer, built on Radiant Digital's Applied Intelligence Architecture, can reason across the fragmented clinical, telehealth, monitoring, claims, transportation, public health, and social systems that characterize rural Nevada healthcare, surfacing cross-system insights, detecting elevated risk, and recommending actions that no single system can produce independently.
The prototype shares the same shell, navigation, and AI capability framework as prior Radiant engagements but is contextualized with five rural Nevada health personas, each mapped to a cross-system intelligence use case aligned to the RAIN proposal and the Rural Health Transformation Program priorities: maternal health, chronic disease management, rural operations and access, population health and equity, and data interoperability.

Shared Root-Cause Thread
The prototype's cross-system intelligence is dramatized through a single root-cause event that cascades differently through all five personas, the same narrative technique used in prior Radiant prototypes. The event:

Battle Mountain Critical Access Hospital labor & delivery closureOn March 1, 2026, the labor and delivery unit at a frontier critical access hospital in Humboldt County (modeled on the Winnemucca service area) suspended obstetric services due to staffing loss. The nearest delivering hospital is now 115 miles away. This single event cascades across every persona: maternal patients lose local prenatal and delivery access, EMS and NEMT transport demand spikes, the same rural clinic's chronic disease patients lose continuity as providers are reassigned, telehealth demand surges, SDOH and transportation barriers compound, and referral and RPM data feeds show the ripple. Nevada is one of only eight states without obstetric services in more than two-thirds of its rural hospitals.

Daily Reality
Dr. Delgado-Reyes leads clinical care across a frontier critical access hospital network serving Humboldt and Pershing Counties, where about 65 percent of Nevadans live in a federally designated primary care health professional shortage area. When the labor and delivery unit at the Battle Mountain critical access hospital suspended obstetric services on March 1, 2026, the nearest delivering hospital became 115 miles away. The gap: prenatal records live in the clinical EHR, risk scores in the maternal health registry, transport capacity in EMS dispatch, and telehealth availability in a separate scheduling system. No single system shows which pregnant patients are now at risk of inadequate prenatal care, an emergency-department delivery, or a birth before arrival. Her team manually cross-references spreadsheets to find at-risk patients, and rural Nevadans already face higher rates of chronic conditions that compound pregnancy risk. With Nevada one of only eight states without obstetric services in more than two-thirds of its rural hospitals, she needs cross-system visibility to intervene before risk escalates.

Daily Reality
Sarah leads care coordination for chronic disease patients across a three-county rural clinic network where residents face higher rates of diabetes and heart disease than the state average (Nevada diabetes prevalence is 12.4 percent, above the national rate). Her team monitors CHF, COPD, diabetes, and hypertension patients to prevent avoidable hospitalizations. The gap: clinical data lives in the EHR, device readings in the RPM platform, medication fills in pharmacy claims, and social barriers in SDOH data, but no single system tells her which patient is deteriorating right now. When the Battle Mountain obstetric closure pulled two providers into coverage gaps, several of her chronic disease patients lost their regular provider, and she cannot easily see who fell through. Her coordinators spend roughly 40 percent of their time manually stitching together data across systems to build outreach lists, time that should go to patients.

Daily Reality
